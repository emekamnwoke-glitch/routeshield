# Glossary

Terms are defined here when they are used in a specific sense in this repository. Where a term has a general meaning and a RouteShield meaning, both are given, because the gap between them is usually where misunderstanding starts.

---

## Domain terms

**Affected vehicle** — A vehicle whose remaining trip intersects a disruption footprint. Distinct from a *delayed* vehicle, which is behind schedule for any reason. RouteShield acts on affected vehicles only.

**Contingency route** — A pre-computed and pre-approved alternative stop sequence for a known corridor. Because approval happened in advance and deliberately, a contingency route may activate automatically without violating P-1.

**Control room** — The operational function with authority to alter services in flight. Modelled in Stage Two as the fictional Reference Operations Control Centre.

**Controller** — The human who approves or rejects a recommendation. The accountable actor under P-1.

**Disruption** — An unplanned condition preventing normal operation over part of the network. Represented in this system as a geographic footprint plus a time window — a simplification registered as [A-007](../02-stage-two-reference-implementation/assumptions.md#a-007) and known to model static disruptions better than moving ones.

**Disruption footprint** — The geographic extent of a disruption, expressed as a region affecting one or more road segments.

**Hold** — A decision to keep a service on its planned route and wait, rather than reroute. A legitimate outcome, not a failure to decide.

**Recommendation** — A ranked alternative stop sequence, presented to a controller with the reasoning that produced it. Under P-1, a recommendation is not an instruction until approved.

**Reroute** — A decision to alter a vehicle's remaining stop sequence, with a revised path between stops.

**Reversion** — Returning a rerouted service to its planned route once the disruption clears. Operationally as hard as the reroute and frequently forgotten in designs of this kind.

**Skipped stop** — A stop on the planned sequence that a rerouted vehicle will not serve. Tracked as an explicit cost with a named beneficiary (P-6), never as a silent side effect.

**Split** — A decision to divide a service, so that part of the route continues to be served from each side of a disruption.

**Stop criticality** — The consequence of skipping a stop, distinct from its footfall. A stop with poor alternatives and dependent passengers is critical even when lightly used (P-7). Currently unmodelled for want of data, and declared as such.

## Architecture terms

**ADM** — Architecture Development Method. The lifecycle structure this project's method is *inspired by*. See [ADR-0001](../05-architecture-decisions/adr-0001-tailored-togaf-inspired-method.md).

**ADR** — Architecture Decision Record. Immutable once accepted; changed by supersession.

**Architecture requirement** — A requirement on the architecture itself, derived from a business requirement. Identified `AR-nnn`.

**Baseline** — The current agreed state of an architecture artefact. The thing a change is a change *from*.

**Building block** — A reusable component of capability, defined at a level of abstraction above any specific technology.

**Dispensation** — A deliberate, justified, time-bound violation of a principle or standard. A legitimate governance outcome; an undocumented violation is not.

**Target architecture** — The intended future state, as opposed to the baseline.

**Traceability** — The recorded chain from business requirement through to test evidence. Required to be complete in both directions: no requirement without a test, no component without a requirement.

**Transition architecture** — An intermediate state that is itself coherent and deployable, between baseline and target.

## Classification terms

Defined fully in the [Fact / Assumption Model](../02-stage-two-reference-implementation/fact-vs-assumption-model.md).

**FACT** — Supported by the baseline proposal or a citable public source. A fact drawn from the proposal is a fact *about the proposal*, not about the world.

**ASSUMPTION** — Supposed in order to build the fictional implementation; not known to reflect any real system. Identified `A-nnn`.

**DESIGN DECISION** — A choice among alternatives, constrained by the project's own limits rather than by missing knowledge.

**FUTURE CONSIDERATION** — Necessary for a real implementation, deliberately not addressed here. Declared rather than silent.

**Fictional reference system** — An invented external system standing in for a real one that cannot be observed. Always labelled at the point of use.

**Synthetic data** — Data generated by this repository's own pipeline. Labelled inside the file, not only by its directory, so it cannot be mistaken for real data once separated from its folder.

## Technical terms

**AVL** — Automatic Vehicle Location. The general category of fleet position tracking.

**GTFS** — General Transit Feed Specification. An open format for scheduled public transport data: routes, stops, sequences, shapes, timetables.

**GTFS-Realtime** — The companion specification for live data: vehicle positions, trip updates, service alerts. Used here as the model for fictional interface shapes, keeping the fiction disciplined (P-4).

**SIRI** — Service Interface for Real Time Information. A European standard for real-time public transport information exchange. Used, like GTFS-Realtime, as a shape to imitate rather than a system to integrate with.

**Stop sequence** — The ordered list of stops a trip serves. The primary object RouteShield modifies.

## Identifier prefixes

| Prefix | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `BR-nnn` | Business requirement |
| `AR-nnn` | Architecture requirement |
| `FR-nnn` | Functional requirement |
| `NFR-nnn` | Non-functional requirement |
| `US-nnn` | User story |
| `TC-nnn` | Test case |
| `ADR-nnn` | Architecture decision record |
| `A-nnn` | Assumption |
| `PA-n` | Project-level assumption (charter) |
| `SA-n` | Scope-bounding assumption |
| `R-nnn` | Risk |
| `P-n` | Architecture principle |
| `O-n` | Project objective |
| `C-n` | Project constraint |

Identifiers are stable. Once published, they are never reused and never renumbered.
