## Context

The situation that makes a decision necessary. What is true, what has changed, what constrains the choice. Written so that a reader arriving in two years understands the world this decision was made in, without needing to reconstruct it.

## Problem

The question being decided, stated as a question. If it cannot be phrased as a question with more than one defensible answer, it is not a decision and does not need an ADR.

## Decision

The option selected, stated plainly and without hedging.

## Rationale

Why this option beat the others — against the principles, the requirements and the constraints in play. Name the principle that decided it where one did. If the decision was close, say it was close and say what would tip it.

## Alternatives rejected

For each rejected option: why, and — importantly — **what would have to change for it to become the right answer.** That last part is what makes an ADR useful later, when conditions have moved.

## Review trigger

The condition under which this decision should be revisited. Not a date; a circumstance. "If X becomes true, reopen this."
